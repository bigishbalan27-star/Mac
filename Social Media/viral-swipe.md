# Viral swipe file: UK Malayali family creators

Collected 19 Nov 2026 from YouTube Shorts, the latest 30 shorts per channel.
Each short is ranked by its **outlier multiple**: views ÷ that channel's median views.
At about 3× or more, the short beat its account's normal; under 1.5× is an ordinary day.

**Limits of this sample.** It covers 60 shorts from 12 accounts (each account's top 3 and bottom 2).
The "hook" column is each short's **title**, not its spoken first line: YouTube's bot check
blocked downloading captions. Small accounts swing a lot, so a 5× short on a
600-subscriber channel is weaker evidence than a 5× on a 7,000-subscriber one. Read the patterns below as directions to test, not proof.

## What is over-indexing

1. **A person or a feeling in the hook, not a place.** In 12 of the 16 outliers the title names a relationship
   (husband/father, daughter, a child's birthday, God, "Gen Alpha" kids) or a feeling. Only 2 name a place.
2. **Kerala life lived in the UK.** Onam preps, a Kannur hometown reel, "did you learn Malayalam?",
   laddu and garden harvests: something from home, made or done here.
3. **Homegrown harvest** is the smallest finding: just 2 outliers, on 2 different small accounts (5.6× and 3.6×).

## What sits at the bottom (0.0–0.3× median)

Mostly **a place or an event name with no person in it**: "Guid Neighbours Day parade",
"Must visit place in the UK", "Sunflower 🌻", "Family time hits different · Sandbanks, Poole",
"Is this castle worth ₹4200?", a forest walk, the ocean. **This is your niche's default format,
and it is the one that flops.** The place reels that do work in your own insights (Megali Ammos,
Little Venice) open on a striking first frame, and that pattern agrees with this.

## Unclassified

All 60 rows were unclassified. Titles aren't spoken hooks, and the formula patterns in `ig-reel/hooks.json`
are English-only. One shape repeats and is missing from it:
- **A Manglish one-liner about kids or a family role**: "Gen Alpha-ye raise ചെയ്യുന്നത്…", "Malayalam പഠിച്ചോ?",
  "When Daddy's in charge". Code-switching is likely part of why it lands with this audience.

## Your version (UK Mallu dad · family travel)

1. **Person first, place second.** Not "Lake District trip" but
   *"When Kerala dad plans the weekend trip 😅"*, then show the place.
2. **Raising UK kids, Kerala rules.** *"UK-il valarunna pillere Kerala style-il valartha…"*:
   one line, mixed Malayalam and English, about the gap between how you grew up and how they're growing up.
3. **A Kerala moment in a UK place.** *"Onam sadya in a UK car park picnic"* or
   *"Appa's first harvest in a British garden"*. Something from home, in a place from here.

Your car reel (4–5× your normal reach) fits #1 if the hook is about you, for example "Driven by passion."
